[F73](facts.md#f73) checks the candidate that adds array and hash mutation sites as writers to F72's rule.
It admits the witness values of 52 of F72's 56 losing rows. Still, 21 rows lose real values, 17 of them new:
13 through routing or missing contributions, 6 in container contents and 2 others. Its equality gains are not
sound gains, and the sampled census bounds the loss rate only loosely.

[F74](facts.md#f74) names the cause of Mastodon's remaining schedule variance: a reader of absence inside a cycle.
A class guard turned a not-yet-computed argument into a Hash, and a six-slot cycle kept that premature value in
some schedules only. With Pending kept pending at the guard, all ten Mastodon tables agree: on F73's candidate
across four schedules, and on a port to upstream main across twelve. The change costs precision in 24 rows and
leaves F73's losses and twelve structural identities open.

[F75](facts.md#f75) builds the controller-binding table once, after completion, from completed method bodies.
On top of F74's guard, all four apps reach 10/10 equal tables, and Forem and Chatwoot no longer vary. The
independent check corrected the binding audit: 482 rows change in meaning, 472 bindings are added and 104 known
values lose precision, 95 of them in Chatwoot. Both the old and the new table miss 45 bindings that Ruby reaches
through calls inside hash arguments or string interpolation. That gap predates the change and stays open.

[F76](facts.md#f76) makes the send catalog wait while an operand is entirely Pending. The 21 catalog strictness
failures pass, all four apps keep 10/10 equal tables, and no value set changes. It costs 3.6–7.7% more Pending at
the completion barrier, and one Chatwoot reason row's provenance now depends on the schedule. The other laws
still have 916 FAIL and 123 UNKNOWN rows, and older catalog gaps such as `Int + Float` typed `Int` remain.

[F77](facts.md#f77) repairs 13 of F73's 21 losing rows on top of F75. Per-class routes and class-body call sites fix
10, and String parameter keys fix 3. All four apps keep 10/10 equal tables. Every narrowing only drops Unresolved;
per-class routes add an Unresolved arm to 1,962 rows. Analysis is 12–22% slower on the larger apps, and 8 of
F73's losses remain.

## October 22 correction

[F77](facts.md#f77) overstated its repairs. Class-body sites (5 rows) and String parameter keys (3 rows) are the
substantive ones: 8 of F73's 21 losses. Per-class routes' 5 repairs and 1,962 widenings all sit in parameter rows
of methods with no analyzer body, and run alone the flag changes no other table, so no analyzer body or emitter
reads them. Their benefit and their cost are table-only, and 53 of the 2,034 widenings remain once such rows are
dropped. F73's census counted rows that nothing reads, so 13 of its rows still exclude a witness or sit in unread
tables.

[F78](facts.md#f78) adds four flags on top of F77. Sidekiq rows, render bindings, ivar call joins and memo
aliases repair 6 more of F73's rows, but five pass only through an Unresolved arm, and C575's value is never
read. Counted by reader, 12 of the 21 rows now have reader-visible repairs; C1376 and C1465 remain. Render
bindings expose an old schema-ingestion bug and add 4 Forem losses, and ivar call joins widen 1,528 rows. A
census by reader finds 14.6% of F73's narrowings unread, yet the bound on reader-visible rows is 48.0%: it is
wide for lack of proofs, not because of unread rows.

[F79](facts.md#f79) supplies proofs. An independent checker reads the pinned source with Prism and proves 1,922
of F73's 4,652 narrowed rows sound, with no known loss among them. It also shows that two of F73's hand proofs
rest on the analyzer's own IR, which corrects F73's bound to 49.5%. Closing the proved rows moves the bound only
to 46.9%, because passing witnesses never close a row. Getting under 10% needs caller enumeration and returns
across methods.

[F80](facts.md#f80) shows that upstream main itself depends on order. A code audit predicted ten shapes, and
five hold in small Rails programs: an ivar written with `[]=` before another method's Hash literal, a model
calling a nil-returning model in another file, the same within one model split across files, a `helper_method`
shared by two controllers, and a view ivar written by two `before_action` filters. For the first three the
swapped order matches CRuby; for the last two neither order matches Rails. The 1,272 generated programs of the
milestone-3 comment had none of these shapes, so its "none yet" no longer holds.
